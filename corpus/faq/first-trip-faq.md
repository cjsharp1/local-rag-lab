# Your First Canoe Trip: Questions and Answers

Never been on a canoe trip before? Good. Plenty of our regular customers started exactly where you are. This page answers the questions first-timers ask us most, in plain terms.

## Choosing a trip

### Which route should we pick for a first trip?

The **Ashby Lake Loop**. It starts and ends at our dock on Ashby Lake, so there is no drive and no shuttle: you load your boat, push off and paddle away, and you finish right back where your car is parked.

The loop is **9 miles** over **2 days and 1 night**, rated **beginner**. It has three portages, all easy:

| # | From → To | Length (rods) | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ashby Lake → Mink Lake | 22 | Easy | |
| 2 | Mink Lake → Otter Pond | 38 | Easy | Longest portage on the loop |
| 3 | Otter Pond → Ashby Lake | 30 | Easy | |

That gives you a taste of everything, paddling, portaging, setting up camp, cooking on a stove, without long days or hard carries. The route guide for the Ashby Lake Loop has the full description.

### What if we want something longer?

Do the Ashby Lake Loop first, or at least take an honest look at how your group handles a full day of paddling and carrying. After that, the Fern Creek Ramble is a common next step. Ask at the counter and we will help you match a route to your group.

## Portages and maps

### What is a portage?

A portage is a trail between two lakes, or around a rapid or a beaver dam, where you carry your canoe and gear over land. The Kettle Basin Canoe Area has no roads inside it, so portages are how you get from one lake to the next.

On a portage, one person usually carries the canoe upside down on their shoulders, resting on the yoke, and the others carry the packs. If your group cannot carry everything in one trip, you walk the trail twice with loads (and once more empty), which takes about three times as long. Pack so you can make each portage in one trip if you can.

### What is a rod?

A **rod** is the traditional unit for measuring portages. **One rod is 16.5 feet**, roughly the length of a canoe, and **320 rods make one mile**. So a 38-rod portage is a little over 600 feet, and a 160-rod portage is half a mile.

### How do I read a portage table?

Every one of our route guides includes a portage table with the same columns:

- **#**: the portage's number, in the order you reach it on the route.
- **From → To**: the lakes (or the feature) at each end of the trail.
- **Length (rods)**: how long the trail is. Divide by 320 to get miles.
- **Difficulty**: easy, moderate or difficult. Difficulty reflects hills, mud, rocks and footing, not just length.
- **Notes**: anything worth knowing, like a steep landing or a muddy stretch.

Look first at the longest portage on the route. If that one sounds like more than your group wants to carry, pick an easier route.

## On the water

### How long is a day of paddling?

Shorter than you might think, and that is a good thing. A relaxed beginner pace is about 2 miles an hour on calm water, and portages, breaks, lunch and finding a campsite all take time. On a first trip, plan on paddling for four to six hours a day and getting to camp by mid-afternoon. Campsites are first come, first served, so arriving early also gives you a better choice of sites.

Wind matters more than distance. A headwind can cut your speed in half, and big waves on open water are a real danger. If it is too windy to cross a lake safely, wait. Wind is often calmer early in the morning and in the evening.

### Do we have to wear life jackets?

Children under 13 must wear one whenever the boat is underway, and we strongly recommend that everyone wear one at all times on the water. Every boat rental comes with one PFD per person. See the PFD sizing guide for fit.

### What if someone gets hurt?

There is no cell service past Ashby Lake, so a phone will not help you once you are out. Rent a **satellite messenger** with your gear. It can send an **SOS** to emergency responders with your location, even from the middle of the Canoe Area. Press SOS only for a genuine emergency, such as a serious injury or someone who cannot travel.

Also bring a basic first-aid kit, and leave your route and expected exit date with someone at home. For small problems, like a blister or a sore shoulder, slow down, rest, and change the plan. Shortening a trip is always an option.

## Before you go

### What do we need to bring?

We can rent you nearly everything, from tents and packs to stoves and sleeping bags, or outfit you completely. Our packing list covers personal items like clothing and rain gear. Remember that cans and glass bottles are not allowed in the Canoe Area.

### Anything else first-timers forget?

Get your permit sorted before your trip date, check the weather, and bring rain gear even if the forecast is sunny. And ask us questions. We would much rather talk through your plan at the counter than have you guess.
