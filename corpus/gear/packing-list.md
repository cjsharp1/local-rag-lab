# Packing List for a Kettle Basin Canoe Trip

This is the packing list we hand out at the Paddle Section Supply desk. It assumes a canoe or kayak trip of two days or more in the Kettle Basin Canoe Area, staying at designated campsites. Items marked **(rental)** can be rented from us; items marked **(included)** come with every boat rental. Everything else you bring from home.

Pack light. Every item you bring gets carried across every portage, sometimes twice.

## How to pack: the portage pack system

Canoe trippers in the Kettle Basin carry gear in a few large packs rather than many small bags. Fewer pieces means fewer trips across each portage and less to forget at a landing.

A typical tandem canoe trip uses:

- **One Portage Pro 80** for the tent, sleeping bags, sleeping pads, and camp clothes
- **One Portage Pro 60** for the kitchen, stove, and day items
- **One food barrel (60 L, with harness)** or a bear-resistant canister for all food and scented items

The goal is one canoe trip across a portage with the boat, plus one trip with the packs, or a single trip if both paddlers can each take a pack and one carries the canoe. Line every pack with a heavy plastic liner. Canoe packs are tough, but they are not waterproof.

For kayaks, use several smaller dry bags that fit through the hatches instead of portage packs.

## Food storage: bear-resistant containers

Bear-resistant containers are **required on Heron Lake, Gull Lake, and Cutfoot Lake**. This requirement is new for 2026. If your route camps on any of those lakes, as the Heron Chain Loop does, you must store food in a bear-resistant container there; hanging food is not enough. On other lakes you may either use a container or hang your food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk.

A bear-resistant canister holds roughly three days of food for one person, so plan accordingly for larger groups or longer trips. A food barrel keeps food dry and organized and carries well on portages. Many groups bring both.

## Boat and paddling

- [ ] Canoe or kayak **(rental)**
- [ ] Paddles: 2 per canoe, 1 per kayak seat **(included)**
- [ ] One PFD per person, each with a whistle **(included)**
- [ ] Yoke pads **(included)**
- [ ] 50-ft rope **(included)**
- [ ] Spare paddle **(rental)**
- [ ] Bailer or sponge
- [ ] Map of your route in a waterproof case
- [ ] Compass
- [ ] Your overnight permit (the permit holder must be in the group)

## Shelter and sleep

- [ ] Tent: Ridgeback 2 (4.8 lb) or Ridgeback 4 (8.6 lb) **(rental)**
- [ ] Sleeping bag, 30°F rating **(rental)**
- [ ] Sleeping pad **(rental)**
- [ ] Small camp pillow, or a stuff sack filled with clothes
- [ ] Tarp and cord for a rain shelter over the kitchen

## Kitchen

- [ ] StormCell 2 stove **(rental)**
- [ ] Isobutane fuel canisters (sold at the store; see our stove and fuel guide)
- [ ] Lighter and waterproof matches
- [ ] Cook pot with lid and a pot grip
- [ ] Bowl, mug, and spoon per person
- [ ] Water filter **(rental)**
- [ ] Two water bottles per person
- [ ] Collapsible water bag for camp
- [ ] Small biodegradable soap and scrub pad
- [ ] Trash bag (pack out everything you pack in)

Wash dishes at least 150 ft from water and keep all soap out of lakes and streams. Cans and glass bottles are not allowed in the area, so repack food and drinks into reusable containers or bags before you leave home.

## Food

- [ ] Breakfasts, lunches, and dinners for every day, plus one extra day's worth
- [ ] Trail snacks within reach for each paddling day
- [ ] Hot drinks
- [ ] Everything repacked into bags or plastic containers (no cans, no glass)
- [ ] All food and scented items in the food barrel or bear-resistant canister

Not sure how to plan meals? Our complete outfitting package includes the food.

## Clothing

Weather in the Kettle Basin changes fast. Pack layers that work wet, and avoid cotton.

- [ ] Quick-dry paddling shirt and pants or shorts
- [ ] Warm mid layer (fleece or synthetic jacket)
- [ ] Rain jacket and rain pants
- [ ] Warm hat and sun hat
- [ ] Light gloves
- [ ] Two pairs of wool or synthetic socks, plus one dry pair kept only for camp
- [ ] Wet shoes for landings and portages (closed-toe, with grip)
- [ ] Dry camp shoes
- [ ] Dry set of camp clothes, sealed in a waterproof bag
- [ ] Underwear for every few days
- [ ] Sunglasses with a retaining strap

## Safety and navigation

- [ ] Satellite messenger with SOS **(rental)**. There is no cell service past Ashby Lake.
- [ ] First aid kit, including blister care and any personal medications
- [ ] Headlamp with spare batteries
- [ ] Knife or multitool
- [ ] Repair kit: duct tape, cord, safety pins, tent pole splint
- [ ] Sunscreen and lip balm
- [ ] Insect repellent and a head net
- [ ] Emergency fire starter

## Personal and hygiene

- [ ] Toothbrush and small toothpaste (store with food at night)
- [ ] Toilet paper in a sealed bag
- [ ] Small trowel for when no latrine is near; bury waste 150 ft from water
- [ ] Hand sanitizer
- [ ] Quick-dry towel
- [ ] ID and the credit card used for your rental deposit (leave other valuables at home)

## Optional

- [ ] Camp chair or seat pad
- [ ] Book or journal
- [ ] Camera in a dry bag
- [ ] Binoculars
- [ ] Playing cards

Leave behind: drones (prohibited in the area), speakers, and anything you would not want to carry over a long portage. Quiet hours run 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

## Before you leave the dock

- [ ] Every pack lined and closed
- [ ] Food barrel or canister sealed
- [ ] Bear-resistant container packed if you will camp on Heron, Gull, or Cutfoot lake
- [ ] PFDs fitted for everyone
- [ ] Permit in hand
- [ ] Someone at home knows your route and your exit day
