# PFD Sizing and Fit

A personal flotation device (PFD) only works if it fits and if you are wearing it. Every boat rental from Paddle Section Supply includes one PFD per person, so there is nothing extra to rent. This guide explains how we size them, how to check the fit, and the rules for children on the water in the Kettle Basin Canoe Area.

## One PFD per person, included

When you pick up a canoe or kayak, we issue one PFD for each person in your group at no added charge. Tell the desk how many adults, youth, and children are coming when you reserve, along with rough chest sizes for adults and weights for kids, and we will set aside the right sizes. We fit everyone at the dock before you launch.

Every PFD we issue has a whistle attached to it. Leave it there. A whistle carries much farther over water than a voice, and three blasts is the standard call for help.

## Sizing table

Adult PFDs are sized by chest measurement. Youth, child, and infant PFDs are sized by body weight.

| Size | Fits | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| Adult S/M | 30–40 in chest | chest circumference |
| Adult L/XL | 40–52 in chest | chest circumference |
| Adult XXL | 52–56 in chest | chest circumference |
| Youth | 50–90 lb | body weight |
| Child | 30–50 lb | body weight |
| Infant | under 30 lb | body weight |

### Measuring chest size

Wrap a soft tape measure around the fullest part of your chest, under the arms, while wearing the kind of layer you will paddle in. Breathe normally. If you fall right on a boundary, such as 40 in, try both sizes at the dock and keep the one that passes the fit test below.

### Between youth and adult

Teens who weigh more than 90 lb move into adult sizing by chest measurement. Most will fit an Adult S/M. Size by fit, not by age.

## The fit test

A PFD that is too loose can ride up over your face in the water, which is exactly when you need it to stay put. Do this test every time someone puts on a PFD at the start of a trip:

1. **Put it on and close everything.** Zip the front and buckle every buckle.
2. **Snug the straps.** Pull the side straps first, then the shoulder straps, until the PFD is snug but you can still breathe deeply and swing your arms in a full paddling stroke.
3. **Lift at the shoulders.** Have a partner grab the PFD at both shoulders and pull straight up. It should not ride above your chin or your ears. If it does, tighten the straps and test again. If it still rides up, go down a size.
4. **Check your stroke.** Sit in the boat and take a few strokes. The PFD should not chafe under the arms or push up into your jaw.

For children, the lift test matters even more. A child's PFD should stay in place when lifted at the shoulders, and the child's chin and ears should not slip through. Child and infant PFDs have a crotch strap; always fasten it.

## Rules for wearing a PFD

### Children under 13

Children under 13 must wear a PFD while underway. That means any time the boat is moving, from leaving the landing to touching the next shore. This is not optional, and our staff will not launch a boat carrying a child under 13 who is not wearing a properly fitted PFD.

### Adults

Adults must have a PFD in the boat, but we strongly recommend wearing it whenever you are on the water. Cold water and wind can turn a capsize into an emergency fast, and it is nearly impossible to put on a PFD after you are in the water. Wear it zipped, not draped open.

## Wearing a PFD on big water and in wind

Open lakes in the Kettle Basin can build steep waves quickly in the afternoon. On any crossing longer than a few minutes, everyone in the boat should be wearing their PFD, fully buckled. Kayakers on routes such as Raven Narrows Big Water should wear theirs the entire time they are on the water; a kayak capsize in wind leaves you in the water immediately.

## Using your PFD in camp and on portages

- **On portages,** wear your PFD or strap it to the outside of a pack. Don't leave it behind at the landing; PFDs are easy to forget, and a lost PFD is charged under our damage and loss policy.
- **In camp,** a PFD makes a decent seat cushion or pillow booster. Keep it out of direct fire heat and away from sparks.
- **At night,** stow PFDs under the overturned canoe so they stay dry.

## Caring for a PFD

- Rinse off sand and mud before return.
- Let it air dry in the shade, not on a hot rock in full sun or next to the campfire.
- Never use a PFD as a boat fender or a kneeling pad on rocks.
- Don't cut, pierce, or retie straps. If a buckle breaks, tell us at return.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] One PFD for every person in the group
- [ ] Adults sized by chest, kids sized by weight
- [ ] All zippers and buckles closed, straps snug
- [ ] Lift test passed: does not ride above the chin
- [ ] Crotch strap fastened on child and infant PFDs
- [ ] Whistle attached to every PFD
- [ ] Children under 13 wearing a PFD whenever the boat is underway
