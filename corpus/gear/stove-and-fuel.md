# Stove and Fuel Guide

Cooking on a stove is faster, cleaner, and more reliable than cooking on a campfire, and during fire restrictions it may be the only way to cook at all. This guide covers the StormCell 2 stove we rent at Paddle Section Supply, the fuel we sell, how fuel travels on our shuttles, and the fire restriction rules in the Kettle Basin Canoe Area.

## The StormCell 2

The StormCell 2 is our rental stove. It is a compact canister stove that:

- **Burns isobutane** from screw-on canisters
- **Boils 1 liter of water in about 4 minutes**
- **Has an on/off valve**, so the flame can be shut off instantly

That on/off valve is what keeps the StormCell 2 legal to use under Stage 2 fire restrictions, when campfires are banned (see below).

The stove folds small enough to nest inside a 1-liter pot with a canister. It comes with a pot support, a folding windscreen, and a storage sack.

### Setting up

1. Pick a flat, stable spot on bare ground or rock, away from the tent and from overhanging branches. The fire grate at your campsite works well as a level base.
2. Unfold the pot supports until they lock.
3. Check that the valve is fully closed (turn it clockwise).
4. Screw the stove onto the canister by hand until it is snug. Do not overtighten.
5. Set up the windscreen around the pot, leaving a gap so the canister does not get hot.

### Lighting and cooking

1. Open the valve a quarter turn and light the burner right away with a lighter or match.
2. Adjust the flame. Full flame boils water fastest; a low flame is better for simmering and saves fuel.
3. Keep a lid on the pot. A lid cuts boil time and fuel use noticeably.
4. Never leave a lit stove unattended, and never cook inside a tent or under the vestibule.
5. When you are done, close the valve completely and let the stove cool before you unscrew it from the canister.

### Cold mornings

Isobutane canisters lose pressure when they are cold, and the flame gets weak. On chilly mornings, keep the next day's canister in your sleeping bag stuff sack or a jacket pocket overnight so it starts warm. Shield the stove from the wind; wind costs more fuel than cold does.

### How much fuel to bring

As a rule of thumb for boiling water for meals and hot drinks, plan on about 50 g of isobutane per person per day. For two people on a three-day trip, one 450 g canister covers it with some to spare. Groups that cook more elaborate meals or simmer for long periods should bring extra. Always carry a little more than you think you need; a partly used canister can come home with you.

### White gas

If you bring your own liquid-fuel stove, white gas may be carried only in approved fuel bottles. Do not carry white gas in drink bottles, jugs, or any other container. Check that the cap seal is in good shape and pack the bottle upright, away from food, in an outside pocket of your pack.

## Fuel on the tow boats

If your trip starts with a tow boat shuttle, such as the run to the Heron Lake landing or to Raven Bay, all stove fuel travels sealed in the boat's fuel locker. That includes isobutane canisters and white gas bottles. Hand your fuel to the boat operator at the dock before loading. The canisters go into the locker sealed and come back to you at the landing. Do not leave fuel inside your packs on a tow boat.

## Fire restrictions

The Kettle Basin Wilderness Office sets fire restrictions for the area when conditions are dry. At all times, fires are allowed only in the fire grate at a designated campsite. When conditions call for it, the Wilderness Office announces one of two stages.

### Stage 1

Under Stage 1 restrictions, campfires are allowed only in grates. Do not build a fire anywhere else, including on rock shores or sand beaches. Stoves may be used as normal.

### Stage 2

Under Stage 2 restrictions, no campfires are allowed at all, not even in the grate. Only stoves with an on/off valve may be used. The StormCell 2 qualifies. Any stove that cannot be shut off instantly with a valve may not be used.

### Finding out the current stage

Fire restrictions can change during your trip, and there is no cell service past Ashby Lake. Ask at the desk when you pick up your permit. If restrictions are announced while you are out, you are expected to follow them once you learn of them, for example from another party or a posted notice at a landing. When in doubt, cook on the stove and skip the fire. The Wilderness Office can also close the area for fire.

## Stove safety

- Cook in the open, never in a tent or vestibule. Burning stoves give off carbon monoxide.
- Keep the stove on stable ground; a tipped pot of boiling water is a common camp injury.
- Keep fuel away from the stove while it is lit, and change canisters only after the stove is off and cool, well away from any flame.
- Do not let the canister get hot. Never surround it with a windscreen.
- Keep children back from the cooking area.
- Never toss canisters, empty or not, into a fire.

## Packing out canisters

Empty and partly used canisters go home with you. Do not leave them at campsites, bury them, or burn them. Bring them back to the store and we will dispose of the empties properly.
