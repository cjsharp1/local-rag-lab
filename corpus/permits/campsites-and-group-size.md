# Campsites and Group Size in the Kettle Basin Canoe Area

This page explains the Kettle Basin Wilderness Office's rules on where you can camp, how many people can travel together, how long you can stay and how to behave at a campsite. It expands on regulations KB-3, KB-5, KB-10 and KB-11. The full list of regulations is on the permits and regulations page.

## Designated campsites only (KB-5)

You may camp only at **designated campsites**. Each designated site has two things: a **steel fire grate** and a **latrine**. If a spot has neither, it is not a campsite, no matter how flat the ground or how many people seem to have stopped there before. Sites are marked on Canoe Area maps, and most can be spotted from the water by a cleared landing.

Camping anywhere else, including on a portage trail, an island without a grate or a beach that "looks fine," is a violation.

### First come, first served

Individual campsites **cannot be reserved**. Your overnight permit gets you into the area on a given date at a given entry point; it does not hold a particular site for you. Sites are taken on a first come, first served basis each day.

That has a few practical consequences:

- **Start looking early.** On busy lakes, the best sites are often taken by mid-afternoon. Plan to stop by 2 or 3 p.m. if you can.
- **Have a backup.** Before you leave in the morning, pick out two or three sites on your map, preferably on different lakes.
- **Do not count on a site being open because it was open last year.** Someone else may have the same idea.
- **An occupied site is taken.** If you see tents, boats or gear, move on. Each campsite holds one group.

## Group size: 9 people and 4 watercraft (KB-3)

A group may have at most **9 people** and at most **4 watercraft**. Both limits apply at the same time: 9 people in 5 boats is over the limit, and so is a group of 10 in 4 boats.

Before 2026 the limit was 10 people. Groups that traveled with 10 in past years now need to plan for 9.

The limit applies everywhere in the area: on the water, on portages and at campsites. It applies to day trips as well as overnight trips.

### Large groups may not split to travel together

If you have more than 9 people, you cannot get around the limit by reserving two permits and traveling as one party. Two permits mean two separate groups. The two groups may not travel together, camp together or share a campsite. In practice that means different entry points or different entry dates, and different routes, so you are not leapfrogging each other on the same lakes.

The limit exists because campsites, portages and landings are sized for small parties. A group of 14 wears out a site fast and makes a lot of noise for everyone nearby.

## How long you can stay (KB-10)

- At most **14 consecutive nights** in the Canoe Area on one trip.
- At most **3 nights at any one campsite**.

After three nights at a site, move to a different campsite. The rule keeps the best sites available to other groups and gives heavily used ground a rest.

## Quiet hours (KB-11)

Quiet hours are **10 p.m. to 6 a.m.** Voices, music and banging pots carry across calm water for a long way, and the group across the lake can hear you clearly. Keep it low after dark, and save the early-morning packing noise until after 6 a.m. if a neighboring site is close.

## Campsite etiquette

The regulations set the minimum. These habits make the area better for the next group, which might be yours next year.

**Arriving.** Land gently and lift boats out of the water rather than sliding them up the rocks. Look around before you unload: check for leaning dead trees over tent pads and pick a pad that drains.

**Tents.** Pitch on existing tent pads. Do not trench around tents, cut branches or dig new spots.

**Fires.** Use only the fire grate, and only when fire restrictions allow (KB-6). Burn small, collect only dead and downed wood, and make sure the fire is out cold before you leave or go to sleep.

**Food.** Store food, trash and scented items by hanging or in a bear-resistant container (KB-7). Containers are required on Heron Lake, Gull Lake and Cutfoot Lake.

**Water and waste.** Use the latrine (KB-8). Wash dishes at least 150 ft from water, with no soap in lakes or streams (KB-12).

**Leaving.** Pack out everything you brought, including food scraps and the bits of trash you find left by others. Scatter unused firewood, and check the site once more for tent stakes, lighters and socks. The goal is a site that looks as if no one camped there.

**Neighbors.** If you must pass close to an occupied site, keep your distance and your voice down. Do not land at or walk through another group's site except in an emergency.

## Questions

If you are not sure whether your group fits the rules, ask at the permit desk before you reserve. It is far easier to change a plan in town than at a campsite on the second night.
