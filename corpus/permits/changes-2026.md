# What Changed for 2026 in the Kettle Basin Canoe Area

The Kettle Basin Wilderness Office made several changes to permits and regulations for the 2026 season. If you have paddled the area before, read this page before you plan, because some habits from past years will now put you over a limit or out of compliance. The full current rules are on the permits and regulations page.

## Summary

| Topic | Before 2026 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Group size (KB-3) | 10 people | 9 people (4 watercraft) |
| EP-14 daily overnight quota | 12 | 8 |
| Bear-resistant containers (KB-7) | optional everywhere | required on Heron, Gull and Cutfoot lakes |
| Permit pickup | same day as entry only | up to 24 hours before entry |
| Leave No Trace video | at pickup | can be watched online before pickup |

## Group size cut from 10 to 9 people

Regulation KB-3 now limits a group to **9 people** and **4 watercraft**. Before 2026 the limit was 10 people.

This is the change most likely to catch returning groups. A party of 10 that has traveled together for years now has to drop to 9 or split into two separate groups. Remember that two groups cannot get around the limit by traveling together on two permits; separate permits mean separate groups that travel and camp apart. See the campsites and group size page for details.

## EP-14 quota cut from 12 to 8

The daily overnight quota for EP-14 has been cut from **12** permits to **8**. With a third fewer permits each day, dates in July and August are likely to fill well ahead of time. If you plan to start there, reserve as early as you can and have a second entry date in mind.

## Bear-resistant containers required on three lakes

Regulation KB-7 still allows food to be stored either by hanging it (at least 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk) or in a bear-resistant container. What is new is that **bear-resistant containers are now required on Heron Lake, Gull Lake and Cutfoot Lake**. On those three lakes, hanging your food no longer meets the rule.

The Wilderness Office made the change after repeated reports of bears getting into hung food on those lakes. If any night of your trip will be spent on Heron, Gull or Cutfoot, bring a container that holds all of your food, trash and scented items. Paddle Section Supply rents bear-resistant canisters; see the current rate sheet.

## Permits can be picked up the day before

You can now pick up your overnight permit **up to 24 hours before your entry**. Before 2026, permits could be picked up only on the day of entry.

The practical benefit is an early start. Pick up the afternoon before, and you can be on the water at first light on your entry day instead of waiting for the desk to open. The permit holder still has to be present at pickup and still has to travel with the group, and the group still has to enter on the date and at the entry point on the permit.

## Leave No Trace video online

The Leave No Trace video that every group watches before a permit is issued can now be **watched online before pickup**. Watch it at home, then tell staff at the desk that your group has seen it. Pickup goes faster, and you can watch it with the whole group rather than whoever made it to the store.

## What did not change

Everything not listed above carries over from past seasons, including the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), the Wilderness Office permit fees, the ban on cans and glass bottles, the stay limits, quiet hours and the drone ban. Check the permits and regulations page for the full list.
