# Kettle Basin Canoe Area: Entry Points

Every trip into the Kettle Basin Canoe Area starts at a numbered entry point. The Kettle Basin Wilderness Office uses the entry point code on permits, quota lists and shuttle bookings, so it helps to know both the code and the name. This page is the reference for both.

An overnight permit names one entry point and one entry date. During the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), each entry point allows a fixed number of overnight permits per day. Once that number is reached for a date, no more overnight permits are issued for that entry point on that date.

## The entry points

### EP-01 — Ashby Lake

Ashby Lake is the entry point at our own dock in Ashby Landing. You can load your boat, walk down the dock and be paddling within minutes, with no drive and no shuttle. It has the largest daily quota in the area, 14 overnight permits, and it is the start and finish of the Ashby Lake Loop, our usual recommendation for a first trip.

### EP-03 — Birch Portage

Birch Portage is a drive-in entry point. As the name suggests, your trip begins with a carry from the parking area to the first lake, so pack to make that first portage in as few trips as possible. With a quota of 6, it is one of the quieter entry points.

### EP-07 — Fern Creek

Fern Creek can be reached by car on gravel Forest Road 412, or by our van shuttle if you would rather not drive the gravel. The creek is narrow and winding, with beaver dams to pull over. It is the starting point of the Fern Creek Ramble. Quota: 8.

### EP-09 — Loon Hollow

Loon Hollow is a drive-in entry point for day use only. No overnight permits are issued for it. It is a good choice for an afternoon paddle when you want a short drive and a quiet shoreline. The kiosk at the landing has the forms day groups need.

### EP-11 — Cedar Flowage

Cedar Flowage is a drive-in entry point onto a broad, shallow flowage with plenty of channels to explore. Its quota of 10 makes it easier to get on a busy weekend than some of the smaller entry points.

### EP-14 — Heron Lake

Heron Lake has no road. The only way in is our tow boat, and the tow from our dock takes about 50 minutes. The daily overnight quota is 8, down from 12 before 2026, so reserve early. Heron Lake is the start of the Heron Chain Loop. Bear-resistant food containers are required on Heron Lake under the Wilderness Office's food storage rule.

### EP-18 — Raven Bay

Raven Bay can be reached by tow boat or by a rough road that is best left to high-clearance vehicles. It opens onto big, open water where wind is the main hazard. It is the starting point of the Raven Narrows Big Water route, which suits kayaks as well as canoes. Quota: 10.

### EP-22 — Upper Granite River

Upper Granite River is reached by our van shuttle. It is the put-in for the Granite River Run, an advanced one-way trip with rapids and long portages. Most groups pair it with a pickup at the Granite River Take-out. Quota: 6.

### EP-25 — Granite River Take-out

The Granite River Take-out is an exit point only. You cannot start a trip here, and it has no quota. It is where groups finishing the Granite River Run meet the van.

### EP-27 — Kettle Lake

Kettle Lake is a drive-in entry point and the only lake in the area where motors are allowed, with a 25 hp limit. Expect some motor traffic near the landing. Its quota is 12. Kettle Lake is also the pickup point for groups finishing the Stillwater Traverse.

### EP-31 — Stillwater Lake

Stillwater Lake is reached by our van shuttle and has the smallest quota in the area, 5 overnight permits a day. It is the put-in for the Stillwater Traverse, a long, advanced one-way trip that ends at Kettle Lake.

### EP-34 — Pike Bay

Pike Bay is a drive-in entry point with a quota of 6. It sees fewer groups than the larger entry points and makes a good start for paddlers who plan their own routes.
