# Kettle Basin Canoe Area: Permits and Regulations

The Kettle Basin Canoe Area is a roadless lake district managed by the Kettle Basin Wilderness Office. The Wilderness Office issues all permits and sets the regulations on this page. Paddle Section Supply is an authorized permit-issuing cooperator, which means we can issue your permit at our store in Ashby Landing, but the rules themselves come from the Wilderness Office and apply to every visitor, whether or not you rent from us.

Read this page before you reserve. Most problems we see at the permit desk come from groups that planned a trip around the wrong entry point, the wrong date or too many people.

## Overnight permits

An overnight permit is required for every overnight trip during the permit quota season, which runs from **May 1 through September 30**. Each entry point has a daily limit on how many overnight permits can be issued for it, so popular entry points on popular dates fill up early.

An overnight permit is good for **one entry point and one entry date**. Your group must enter the Canoe Area on the date printed on the permit, at the entry point printed on the permit. You cannot use a permit for a different entry point because your first choice turned out to be windy, and you cannot start a day late because someone missed a ride. If your plans change, you need a different permit, and that depends on what quota is still available.

Once you are inside the area, you may travel wherever your route takes you and exit wherever you planned. The entry point and date control only how you get in.

For a list of entry points, how to reach them and their daily quotas, see the entry points page.

### Who must be on the trip

The **permit holder must be in the group**. The person whose name is on the permit has to be present at pickup and has to travel with the group for the trip. You cannot reserve a permit in your name and hand it off to friends. If the permit holder cannot go, contact the issuing office before your entry date.

### Fees

The Wilderness Office charges:

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Reservation fee | $6 per permit (never refunded) |
| User fee, adult | $16 per adult per trip |
| User fee, youth (under 18) | $8 per youth per trip |

The $6 reservation fee is not refunded under any circumstances, including weather or a change of plans.

Paddle Section Supply issues overnight permits **free with any rental**. If you are not renting from us, we can still issue your permit for a **$10 service fee**. The Wilderness Office fees apply either way.

### Picking up your permit

You may pick up your overnight permit **up to 24 hours before your entry**. Picking up the afternoon before lets you leave at first light on your entry day without waiting at the desk. Bring photo ID for the permit holder.

Before a permit is issued, the group watches the Leave No Trace video, which covers the regulations below. You can watch it online before pickup to save time at the desk; tell staff when you check in.

## Day-use permits

Day-use permits are required for day trips into the Kettle Basin Canoe Area. They are self-issued at the entry point kiosk, so you do not reserve them in advance and you do not need to stop at our store or at the Wilderness Office to get one. If you are paddling in and out on the same day, a day-use permit is the paper you need.

The kiosk is a small roofed board near the landing at each entry point. It holds a box of blank forms, pencils, and a slotted drop box. Fill out a form before you put in. The form asks for the leader's name, the entry point, the date, the number of people and boats in the group, and a rough idea of where you plan to go. Print clearly. Rangers use the forms to know who is out on the water, and on a bad-weather afternoon that list matters.

The form has two parts. Tear along the perforation, drop the top copy in the slot, and keep the bottom copy with your group. Rangers patrol the lakes and portages, and they may ask to see it. If the box is empty, write the same information on any piece of paper, drop it in the slot, and note on your copy that the forms had run out. Staff restock the kiosks regularly, but a busy weekend can empty one.

A day trip is still a trip into the Canoe Area, so every regulation on this page applies. The group size limit applies. The ban on cans and glass bottles applies. Fires are allowed only in the grate at a designated campsite, and only when fire restrictions allow. You may stop at an unoccupied campsite for lunch or a swim, but you may not leave gear there or stay the night. If you decide partway through the day that you want to stay out overnight, you cannot; an overnight trip during the quota season needs an overnight permit, issued in advance, for that entry date.

Some entry points are day use only and have no overnight quota at all. Those are listed on the entry points page. Day trips can also start from any other entry point; the overnight quota does not limit how many day groups use a landing.

Plan your day with daylight and wind in mind. Wind usually builds through the afternoon, and a crossing that was calm at 9 a.m. can be rough by 3 p.m. Tell someone outside the group where you are going and when you expect to be back. Bring a map even for a short outing; shorelines in the area look alike from the water, and more than one group has paddled into the wrong bay on the way back to the landing. Leave the landing as you found it, with boats moved clear of the ramp.

They cost nothing, you need only one per group, and they must be carried in the boat for the whole trip.

## Regulations

These regulations apply to everyone in the Kettle Basin Canoe Area. Each has a code that rangers and staff use. The codes match the summary card handed out with every permit.

### KB-1 — Permit required

An overnight permit is required for overnight trips during the quota season (May 1 – September 30). A day-use permit is required otherwise.

### KB-2 — Enter on your permit's date and entry point

Your group must enter on the date and at the entry point shown on the permit.

### KB-3 — Group size: 9 people and 4 watercraft

A group may have at most **9 people** and at most **4 watercraft**. This applies on the water, on portages and at campsites. Before 2026 the limit was 10 people.

### KB-4 — No cans or glass bottles

Cans and glass bottles are not allowed anywhere in the area. Repackage food and drinks into reusable containers or bags before your trip.

### KB-5 — Camp only at designated campsites

Camp only at designated campsites, which are marked on maps and have a steel fire grate and a latrine.

### KB-6 — Fires only in the grate

Build fires only in the campsite's fire grate, and obey any fire restrictions in effect. Restrictions can limit or ban campfires; check at pickup.

### KB-7 — Food storage

Store all food, trash and scented items either hung at least **12 ft off the ground and 6 ft out from the tree trunk**, or in a bear-resistant container. Bear-resistant containers are **required on Heron Lake, Gull Lake and Cutfoot Lake**; hanging food does not meet the rule on those lakes. The container requirement is new in 2026.

### KB-8 — Human waste

Use the campsite latrine. Away from a latrine, bury human waste at least **150 ft from water**.

### KB-9 — No motors

Motors are not allowed, except on Kettle Lake, where motors are limited to **25 hp**.

### KB-10 — Stay limit

You may stay at most **14 consecutive nights** in the Canoe Area, and at most **3 nights at any one campsite**.

### KB-11 — Quiet hours

Quiet hours are **10 p.m. to 6 a.m.** Sound carries a long way over water.

### KB-12 — Dishwashing

Wash dishes at least **150 ft from water**. No soap of any kind in lakes or streams, including biodegradable soap.

### KB-13 — Drones prohibited

Drones may not be launched, flown or landed in the Canoe Area.

## Violations

Rangers enforce these regulations. A violation can bring a **fine of up to $500** and **removal from the area**. Removal ends your trip on the spot.

If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask at the permit desk before you go. It is much easier to sort out in town than on a portage.
