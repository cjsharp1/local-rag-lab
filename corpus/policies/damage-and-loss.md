# Damage and Loss Policy

Canoe trips are hard on gear, and we expect our boats and equipment to come back looking used. This page explains what counts as normal wear, what we charge when something is damaged or lost, and how we work out those charges. All charges are per item.

## Normal wear is on us

Scratches and scuffs on hulls, worn paddle grips, faded fabric and dirty packs are part of renting gear in a lake district full of rock. We don't charge for any of that. Our staff fix normal wear between rentals, and it is built into our rates.

What we do charge for is damage that needs a real repair, and gear that doesn't come back.

## How we check gear in

When you return, a staff member checks your gear in with you at the dock or the counter. We look over each hull, the seats and yoke, paddles and PFDs, and we set up and inspect tents that look like they've had a hard time. If we find damage, we'll show it to you before you leave.

Gear returned through the after-hours drop is checked in the next morning. If we find damage then, we photograph it and contact you.

Charges are taken from your deposit. Our deposits and payment policy explains how deductions are itemized, with photos, and how quickly the rest of your deposit is released.

### How Kevlar repairs are assessed

A crack or puncture in a Kevlar hull is charged at $450 to $900, depending on the size of the damage and how much of the hull has to be rebuilt. Our repair staff assess the boat after it's cleaned and dried, and we send you the assessment with photos. A small puncture from a single sharp rock sits at the low end of the range; a long crack across the bottom or a hull that has folded on a rock sits at the high end.

### Total loss

A boat is a total loss when it can't be recovered, or when the damage costs more to repair than the boat is worth. The charge is the amount in the table for that model.

## Damage waiver

The damage waiver is an optional add-on you can buy for each boat you rent. It costs $9 per boat per day, and you can buy it at pickup only; it can't be added by phone in advance, partway through a trip, or after you return. With the damage waiver, the most you'll pay for damage to a boat is $250 per boat, no matter what the repair costs.

To see what that means in practice, look at the table above. A crack in a Northline 17 Kevlar hull can run anywhere from $450 to $900. With this coverage in place, your share stops at $250 for that boat. The same goes for any combination of damage to a single boat on the same rental: a broken yoke, a broken seat and a gelcoat gouge on the same canoe together would come to $300, but you'd pay $250. The cap is per boat, so if your group has two canoes and both are damaged, each is capped separately.

It's bought for every day of your rental, from pickup to return, and you add it boat by boat. A group renting a Kevlar canoe and an aluminum canoe can cover just the Kevlar boat if they like. You can't cover only part of the trip, such as just the days you're on the toughest portages.

Who should consider it? We usually suggest it for anyone renting a Kevlar boat, especially on routes with long, rocky portages or rapids, or for paddlers who are new to loading and landing a canoe on rough shorelines. Kevlar is light and fast, but it's brittle when it hits rock hard, and repairs are expensive. Aluminum boats are much tougher and rarely need more than a dent pulled, so many renters decide they don't need it for those.

It doesn't change how check-in works. We still inspect each boat, photograph any damage, and send you the itemized charges, with the cap applied for any covered boat. It also doesn't change your deposit, which is held the same way whether or not you buy it.

Please don't treat it as permission to be rough with a boat. Our care rules in the rental agreement still apply, and careful paddling keeps our fleet in good shape for everyone. Lift the boat clear at landings and portages, load it while it's floating, and keep it tied up or carried well above the water at night.

It does not cover everything. Loss of a boat through negligence is charged in full; that includes a boat left unsecured overnight and a boat left behind at a landing. Damage from towing a boat behind a motorboat is charged in full, since rented gear may not be towed behind a motorboat in the first place. And it applies only to boats: paddles, PFDs, tents and packs, and all other gear, are charged in full at the rates in the table above.

## Lost gear

If something goes missing on your trip, tell us when you return so we can note it on your check-in. If you find it afterward and bring it back in good condition within 30 days, we'll refund the loss charge.

If a boat is lost, for example blown off a campsite in a storm, report it to us as soon as you can and tell us where it was last seen. We'll work with you on recovery before treating it as a total loss.

## Damage in the field

If something breaks on your trip, your first job is to get your group home safely. Use the rope and a bit of tape for a temporary fix if you need to, but please don't make permanent repairs, drill holes or use adhesives on our boats. Bad field repairs often cost more to undo than the original damage. Tell us exactly what happened when you return so we can assess it fairly.
