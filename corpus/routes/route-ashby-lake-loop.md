# Ashby Lake Loop

*A Paddle Section Supply route guide for the Kettle Basin Canoe Area*

## Overview

If you have never spent a night in the Kettle Basin, start here. The Ashby Lake Loop is the gentlest overnight trip we know: you push off from our own dock, paddle a short way up Ashby Lake, cross three short, well-worn portages, camp one night on a quiet pond, and glide back to the dock the next afternoon in time for a hot meal in town.

- **Length:** 2 days / 1 night
- **Distance:** 9 miles
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Start and end:** the Paddle Section Supply dock on Ashby Lake, in Ashby Landing
- **Portages:** 3, all easy
- **Longest portage:** Mink Lake → Otter Pond, 38 rods

This is the route we recommend for first-timers, families with older kids, and anyone trying out a new tent or a new paddling partner before committing to something longer. Nothing on the loop is far from help, and the whole trip stays within a few hours' paddle of our dock.

## Getting there

There is no shuttle and no drive to worry about. You launch right from our dock at 14 Dock Road, Ashby Landing. Pick up your boat and gear at the store (pickup starts at 7 a.m. on your first day), load up at the dock, and go.

**Permit:** During the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), every overnight trip in the Kettle Basin needs an overnight permit from the Kettle Basin Wilderness Office. For this route, your permit must be for the **Ashby Lake entry point**, and you must enter on the date printed on it. We issue permits at the counter. You can pick yours up up to 24 hours before your entry date, which makes an early start on day one easy. Outside the quota season, check with the desk about current permit requirements.

**Returning:** Rental gear is due back by 6 p.m. on your last day. The loop is short enough that most groups are back at the dock by mid-afternoon.

## Day by day

### Day 1: The dock to Otter Pond (about 5 miles)

Paddle north from the dock along the east shore of Ashby Lake. The shoreline is mostly birch and pine, and the town noise fades within the first half hour. Keep the shore on your right; in a south wind this side is noticeably calmer.

After a couple of hours you'll reach the head of a narrow bay with a sign post and a worn landing. This is portage 1, a short, flat carry over to Mink Lake. It's a good place to practice your portage routine: one person takes the canoe, the other takes a pack, and you both come back for the rest if needed.

Mink Lake is small, sheltered, and usually glassy in the evening. Cross it to the far end, where portage 2 leads to Otter Pond. This is the longest carry on the loop, but it's still easy, with a gentle rise and a good gravel tread. Otter Pond has the route's campsites, so plan to stop here for the night. Set up camp, filter water, and enjoy a quiet evening; loons are common on the pond.

### Day 2: Otter Pond back to the dock (about 4 miles)

From the south end of Otter Pond, portage 3 drops back down to Ashby Lake. It comes out in a small cove on the west side of the lake. From there, it's a straightforward paddle south along the west shore back to the dock. If the wind has come up from the south, hug the shore and take breaks in the coves. You should reach the dock comfortably before the 6 p.m. return time.

## Portages

Distances are in rods, the traditional portage measure: 1 mile = 320 rods; 1 rod = 16.5 ft.

| # | From → To | Length (rods) | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ashby Lake → Mink Lake | 22 | Easy | Flat and short; landing at the head of a narrow bay, marked with a sign post |
| 2 | Mink Lake → Otter Pond | 38 | Easy | Longest portage on the loop; gentle rise, gravel tread |
| 3 | Otter Pond → Ashby Lake | 30 | Easy | Downhill to a small cove on the west side of Ashby Lake |

The longest portage on the Ashby Lake Loop is portage 2, Mink Lake → Otter Pond, at 38 rods. None of the three carries has a steep climb or a muddy section in normal conditions, so this is a good route for learning to carry a canoe on your shoulders.

## Campsites

All camping in the Kettle Basin is at designated campsites, each with a fire grate and a latrine. Sites are first come, first served; you can't reserve a specific site.

- **Otter Pond** has 3 designated campsites. The site on the north point is the most popular, with a flat tent area and a good evening view. The two sites on the east shore are more sheltered in a west wind.
- **Mink Lake** has 1 designated campsite on its south shore. It's a good backup if Otter Pond is full when you arrive, though it adds a little paddling on day 2.

If every site is taken, keep going. You may not camp anywhere but a designated site, so leave yourself enough daylight to reach the next one. On a busy summer weekend, aim to be at Otter Pond by early afternoon.

Group size in the Kettle Basin is limited to 9 people and 4 watercraft, and that limit applies at the campsite too. Quiet hours are 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

## Hazards

The Ashby Lake Loop is our easiest route, but it's still wilderness travel.

- **Wind on Ashby Lake.** Ashby Lake is the biggest water on the loop. Afternoon winds can build whitecaps, especially in a long north or south wind. Stay near shore, cross in the morning when you can, and wait it out in a cove if the waves feel too big. Our staff may delay a launch from the dock if the wind is strong.
- **Cold water.** Even in July the water is colder than it looks. Wear your PFD whenever you're on the water; one is included for every person with each boat.
- **Wet rocks at landings.** Portage landings can be slippery. Step out of the canoe carefully, and lift the canoe clear of the rocks rather than dragging it.
- **No cell service.** There's no cell service past Ashby Lake, which means most of this loop. If you want a way to call for help, rent a satellite messenger.
- **Food and animals.** Black bears live throughout the Kettle Basin. Hang your food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk, or use a bear-resistant container. Never keep food in your tent.

## Packing note

Keep it light: this is a one-night trip with short carries, and the less you bring, the more fun the portages are. A Northline 17 Kevlar canoe (44 lb) is the most popular choice and easy for most pairs to carry; a Northline 17 Aluminum (72 lb) is heavier but shrugs off bumps from beginners. One Portage Pro 60 pack per person is usually plenty, plus a food barrel or a bear-resistant canister. A Ridgeback 2 or Ridgeback 4 tent, a StormCell 2 stove, a water filter, rain gear and a warm layer round out the list. Remember that cans and glass bottles aren't allowed in the Kettle Basin, so repack drinks and food at home. For rental rates, see the current rate sheet.
