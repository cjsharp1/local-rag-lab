# Fern Creek Ramble

*A Paddle Section Supply route guide for the Kettle Basin Canoe Area*

## Overview

The Fern Creek Ramble is a small-water loop with a lot of character. You'll wind up a narrow, alder-lined creek, hop a beaver dam, and string together three quiet lakes and a pond before drifting back down Fern Creek to where you started. It's the natural next step after the Ashby Lake Loop: longer days and longer carries, but no big open water.

- **Length:** 3 days / 2 nights
- **Distance:** 16 miles
- **Difficulty:** Beginner–intermediate
- **Start and end:** the Fern Creek entry point landing
- **Portages:** 5
- **Longest portage:** Willow Lake → Fern Hill Lake ("the Fern Hill portage"), 120 rods
- **Special feature:** beaver dams on Fern Creek

## Getting there

The Fern Creek landing is outside town, and you have two ways to reach it.

- **Drive.** The landing is a drive-in entry point on gravel Forest Road 412. The road is passable for ordinary cars in dry weather but can be rutted after rain, so take it slowly. Pick up your gear at our store in Ashby Landing first, then drive out with the canoe on your roof.
- **Van shuttle.** If you'd rather leave your car in town, our van will take you, your boat and your gear to the landing and pick you up at the end of the trip. The van leaves our dock at 8 a.m. and at noon. Reserve the shuttle at least 48 hours ahead. For shuttle costs, see the current rate sheet.

**Permit:** During the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), you need an overnight permit, and for this route it must be for the **Fern Creek entry point**. You must enter at that entry point on the date on your permit. We issue permits at our counter, and you can pick yours up up to 24 hours before your entry date. The Fern Creek entry point has a modest daily quota, so book early for summer weekends.

## Day by day

### Day 1: Fern Creek to Willow Lake (about 6 miles)

From the landing, paddle upstream on Fern Creek. The current is gentle, and the creek meanders through sedge meadows and alder thickets. Watch for beaver lodges along the banks.

About an hour up the creek you'll meet the main beaver dam, which is portage 1, the Fern Creek beaver dam pullover. Pull in to the low spot on the right bank, step out onto the dam carefully, and lift the canoe over. Depending on water levels, you may also meet one or two smaller dams that you can float over or step across without unloading; they change from year to year.

Above the dam the creek narrows. Look for the portage sign on the left bank: portage 2 climbs gently through mixed forest to Willow Lake. Willow Lake is long and narrow, with good campsites on its west shore. Camp here for night one.

### Day 2: Willow Lake to Bittern Pond (about 5 miles)

Today is the hard day, with the route's two longest carries.

Paddle to the north end of Willow Lake to find portage 3, the Fern Hill portage. It's the longest carry on the route, climbing steadily over the shoulder of Fern Hill before dropping to Fern Hill Lake. The trail is rated moderate: it's well marked but rooty in places, so take it at a steady pace and rest at the height of land.

Fern Hill Lake is a clear, rocky-shored lake, a good spot for lunch. From its east end, portage 4 runs through a low, sometimes wet spruce stretch to Bittern Pond. Bittern Pond is shallow and marshy at the edges, with herons and bitterns in the reeds. Its campsite sits on a small rise on the south shore. Camp here for night two.

### Day 3: Bittern Pond back to the landing (about 5 miles)

Portage 5 leaves the southwest corner of Bittern Pond and drops down an easy trail to rejoin Fern Creek below the beaver dam. From there, it's a relaxing downstream paddle back to the landing. If you came by van, be at the landing in time for your scheduled pickup. Rental gear is due back at our store by 6 p.m. on your last day.

## Portages

Portage lengths are given in rods: 1 mile = 320 rods; 1 rod = 16.5 ft.

| # | From → To | Length (rods) | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fern Creek beaver dam pullover | 15 | Easy | Lift over the dam at the low spot on the right bank; footing is uneven |
| 2 | Fern Creek → Willow Lake | 64 | Moderate | Gentle climb through mixed forest; sign on the left bank |
| 3 | Willow Lake → Fern Hill Lake ("the Fern Hill portage") | 120 | Moderate | Longest portage on the route; steady climb over Fern Hill, rooty in places |
| 4 | Fern Hill Lake → Bittern Pond | 105 | Moderate | Low, wet spruce section; expect muddy boots after rain |
| 5 | Bittern Pond → Fern Creek | 48 | Easy | Downhill; rejoins Fern Creek below the beaver dam |

The longest portage on the Fern Creek Ramble is the Fern Hill portage, Willow Lake → Fern Hill Lake, at 120 rods.

## Campsites

All camping is at designated campsites with a fire grate and latrine, first come, first served.

- **Willow Lake** has 4 designated campsites, three on the west shore and one on a small island near the north end. The island site is lovely but exposed in a north wind.
- **Fern Hill Lake** has 2 designated campsites. Use them if you get a late start on day 2 and don't want to tackle portage 4 in the evening.
- **Bittern Pond** has 1 designated campsite on its south shore. If it's taken, return to Fern Hill Lake or continue down to Fern Creek and back toward the landing; you may not camp anywhere but a designated site.

You may stay at most 3 nights at one campsite. Quiet hours are 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. Groups are limited to 9 people and 4 watercraft.

## Hazards

- **Beaver dams.** Dams are slippery and can shift underfoot. Step onto the solid center of the dam, keep three points of contact, and lift the canoe over; never drag it across the sticks.
- **Water levels.** In a dry late summer, parts of Fern Creek can get shallow and you may need to wade short stretches. After heavy rain the creek runs faster and the portage 4 trail gets muddy.
- **Rooty trails.** The Fern Hill portage is long enough that a twisted ankle is a real concern. Wear sturdy shoes, not sandals.
- **Insects.** The creek and pond edges are buggy in June. Bring a head net.
- **No cell service.** There is no cell service on this route. A satellite messenger is a good idea.
- **Food storage.** Hang food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk, or use a bear-resistant container.

## Packing note

With a 120-rod carry and a wet trail on day 2, weight matters. A Northline 17 Kevlar (44 lb) is the best fit for most pairs; solo paddlers love the Kestrel 15 (32 lb) on a creek this tight. Pack into two Portage Pro packs per canoe so you can single-trip the portages if your group is strong. Bring sturdy shoes that can get wet, a head net, a water filter, and a food barrel or bear-resistant canister. No cans or glass bottles. For rental and shuttle rates, see the current rate sheet.
