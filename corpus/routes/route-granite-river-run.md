# Granite River Run

*A Paddle Section Supply route guide for the Kettle Basin Canoe Area*

## Overview

The Granite River Run is a one-way river journey for experienced paddlers. Over five days you'll follow the Granite River downstream through falls, ledges and boulder-choked rapids, past high granite walls and through long, quiet pools. It's the only true river trip in our route guides, and it's demanding: long days, heavy portages, and moving water that deserves respect.

- **Length:** 5 days / 4 nights
- **Distance:** 38 miles
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Start:** the Upper Granite River put-in
- **End:** the Granite River take-out
- **Portages:** 5
- **Longest portage:** "the Long Portage", 410 rods

**Important:** In this guide's plan, every rapid and falls on the route is portaged. We do not recommend running any of them with rental boats. The portages exist for a reason.

## Getting there

This is a one-way trip, so you'll use our van both ways. The van takes you, your boat and your gear from our store in Ashby Landing to the Upper Granite River put-in, and picks you up at the Granite River take-out at the end of day five. The van leaves our dock at 8 a.m. and at noon; we recommend the 8 a.m. departure so you have a full first day on the river. Reserve the shuttle at least 48 hours ahead and confirm your take-out pickup time when you book. For shuttle costs, see the current rate sheet.

**Permit:** During the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), your overnight permit must be for the **Upper Granite River entry point**, and you must enter there on the date printed on the permit. The Granite River take-out is an exit point only, so a permit for the take-out won't work. The Upper Granite River entry point has a small daily quota; reserve early. We issue permits at the counter, and you can pick yours up up to 24 hours before entry.

## Day by day

### Day 1: The put-in to below Upper Falls (about 8 miles)

The river starts narrow and quick below the put-in. Within the first few miles you'll hear Upper Falls before you see it. Stay well to the right as you approach; the portage landing is in a calm eddy on river right, marked with a sign, well above the lip of the falls. Portage 1 is rated difficult: it climbs a steep, rocky bank, crosses a bench of bare granite, and drops down a staircase of rock and roots to the pool below. Take it slowly. Camp below Upper Falls.

### Day 2: Ledge Rapids (about 8 miles)

A long stretch of easy current and flatwater leads to Ledge Rapids, a series of shelves that look runnable from upstream and aren't. Land on river left at the sign and take portage 2, a moderate carry along a path above the ledges. Continue downstream to camp on one of the pools below.

### Day 3: The Long Portage (about 7 miles)

Today is the big one. The river drops into a narrow granite canyon, and the only way around is portage 3, the Long Portage. It's the longest carry on the route and rated difficult: a long climb out of the river valley, an exposed ridge walk, and a steep descent back to the water. Most groups need several hours to move everything across. Start early, keep the group together, and drink plenty of water. Camp at the foot of the Long Portage.

### Day 4: Rock Garden Rapids (about 8 miles)

The river widens and slows below the canyon. In the afternoon you'll reach Rock Garden Rapids, a wide boulder field with no clean line in any water level. Land on river right and take portage 4, a moderate trail through cedar and spruce. Camp on the broad pools downstream.

### Day 5: Lower Falls to the take-out (about 7 miles)

The last rapid is Lower Falls. Land well above it on river left and take portage 5, a moderate carry with a short rocky descent. From the bottom of Lower Falls, it's an easy paddle to the Granite River take-out, where our van will meet you at your scheduled time. Rental gear is due back at our store by 6 p.m. on your last day, so don't linger too long over lunch.

## Portages

Portage lengths are given in rods: 1 mile = 320 rods; 1 rod = 16.5 ft.

| # | From → To | Length (rods) | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upper Falls | 85 | Difficult | River right; steep rocky bank and a rock-and-root staircase down |
| 2 | Ledge Rapids | 40 | Moderate | River left; path above the ledges |
| 3 | "The Long Portage" | 410 | Difficult | Longest portage on the route; long climb, ridge walk, steep descent |
| 4 | Rock Garden Rapids | 120 | Moderate | River right; trail through cedar and spruce |
| 5 | Lower Falls | 60 | Moderate | River left; short rocky descent at the end |

The longest portage on the Granite River Run is the Long Portage, at 410 rods. Nothing else on this river comes close, so budget most of day three for it.

## Campsites

All camping is at designated campsites with a fire grate and latrine, first come, first served. River campsites are fewer and farther apart than lake sites, so plan your days around them.

- **Below Upper Falls:** 2 designated campsites on the pool below the falls.
- **Below Ledge Rapids:** 2 designated campsites on the pools downstream.
- **Foot of the Long Portage:** 1 designated campsite. If it's taken, the next designated site is a few miles downstream, so keep some energy in reserve.
- **Above Rock Garden Rapids:** 2 designated campsites on the broad pools.
- **Above Lower Falls:** 1 designated campsite, a good fallback if day four runs long.

Never camp at a portage landing or on a gravel bar. Groups are limited to 9 people and 4 watercraft. Quiet hours are 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

## Hazards

- **Falls and rapids.** Every falls and rapid on this route is portaged in our plan. Approach each one slowly, stay close to the shore on the portage side, and land well above the drop. Current near a falls is stronger than it looks.
- **High water.** After heavy rain, the river rises fast and portage landings can be swept by current. If the water is high and brown, wait it out in camp.
- **Cold water.** A capsize in moving water is serious. Wear your PFD at all times on the river.
- **The Long Portage.** Fatigue is the main danger. Take breaks, share loads fairly, and watch your footing on the descent.
- **Remote travel.** There's no cell service and no road access between the put-in and the take-out. A satellite messenger is strongly recommended.
- **Food storage.** Hang food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk, or use a bear-resistant container.

## Packing note

This is not a trip for heavy gear. Choose a Northline 17 Kevlar (44 lb) for its light weight on the portages, or the Northline 17 Aluminum (72 lb) if you want a boat that shrugs off rock hits. Pack into Portage Pro 80 packs and bring a spare paddle; losing a paddle on a river is a real problem. Add a satellite messenger, a water filter, a food barrel or bear-resistant canister, and boots that can handle wet granite. For rental and shuttle rates, see the current rate sheet.
