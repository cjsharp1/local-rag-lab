# Heron Chain Loop

*A Paddle Section Supply route guide for the Kettle Basin Canoe Area*

## Overview

The Heron Chain Loop is our favorite intermediate trip, and the one we hear the most stories about afterward. It starts with a tow-boat ride to the roadless Heron Lake landing, then strings five lakes into a loop that brings you back to where you began. Along the way you'll cross the Cutfoot carry, the toughest portage on any of our loop routes, and earn some of the quietest campsites in the Kettle Basin.

- **Length:** 4 days / 3 nights
- **Distance:** 24 miles
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Start and end:** the Heron Lake landing (reached only by our tow boat)
- **Portages:** 5
- **Longest portage:** Split Rock Lake → Cutfoot Lake ("the Cutfoot carry"), 290 rods
- **Special rule:** bear-resistant containers are required on Heron, Gull and Cutfoot lakes

## Getting there

There is no road to Heron Lake. The only way in is our tow boat from the Paddle Section Supply dock on Ashby Lake. Your canoe rides on the tow boat's rack while you sit back and watch the shoreline go by. The tow to Heron Lake takes about 50 minutes.

Tow boats leave our dock at 7 a.m., 10 a.m. and 1 p.m. We recommend the 7 a.m. or 10 a.m. departure so you have time to reach Split Rock Lake on day one. Reserve your tow at least 48 hours ahead, and set your pickup time on day four when you book. Stove fuel rides sealed in the tow boat's fuel locker; hand it to the driver at the dock. For tow-boat costs, see the current rate sheet.

**Permit:** Your permit must be for the Heron Lake entry point, and you must enter on the date printed on it. The Heron Lake entry point has one of the smallest daily overnight quotas in the area, and it was reduced for 2026, so book your permit as early as you can. We issue permits at our counter and you can pick yours up up to 24 hours before your entry date, which is handy for catching the early tow.

## Day by day

### Day 1: Heron Lake to Split Rock Lake (about 6 miles)

The tow boat drops you at the Heron Lake landing. Paddle west along the north shore of Heron Lake to a shallow bay where portage 1 crosses a low, easy neck of land to Little Heron Lake.

Little Heron is a narrow, sheltered lake, a good place for a snack break. At its west end, portage 2 heads to Split Rock Lake. It's a moderate carry with a couple of short climbs. Split Rock Lake is named for the cleft boulder at its east end, which you'll paddle past on the way to camp. Spend night one on Split Rock Lake, and get a good night's sleep: tomorrow is the Cutfoot carry.

### Day 2: The Cutfoot carry to Cutfoot Lake (about 5 miles)

Paddle to the south end of Split Rock Lake to reach portage 3, the Cutfoot carry. This is the longest and hardest portage on the route. The trail climbs steeply from the landing, crosses a ridge, and then descends through a muddy, poorly drained section before reaching Cutfoot Lake. Plan to double-portage unless your group is very strong, and take your time on the descent. Rest stops are fine; there's no prize for speed.

Cutfoot Lake is deep and clear, with steep rocky shores. Camp here for night two. Remember that bear-resistant containers are required on Cutfoot Lake.

### Day 3: Cutfoot Lake to Gull Lake (about 7 miles)

Today is a rest from the hard work. Paddle the length of Cutfoot Lake to portage 4, a moderate carry with a steady grade over to Gull Lake. Gull Lake is the biggest water on the loop, with a long open stretch in the middle, so watch the wind and cross early if you can. Camp on Gull Lake for night three. Bear-resistant containers are required here as well.

### Day 4: Gull Lake back to Heron Lake (about 6 miles)

From the east end of Gull Lake, portage 5 runs to Heron Lake. It's moderate, with a long gentle climb and a short steep pitch near the Heron Lake end. Once you're back on Heron Lake, paddle to the landing to meet the tow boat at your scheduled pickup time. Be there early; the driver can't wait long if other groups are scheduled. Rental gear is due back at our store by 6 p.m. on your last day.

## Portages

Portage lengths are given in rods: 1 mile = 320 rods; 1 rod = 16.5 ft.

| # | From → To | Length (rods) | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heron Lake → Little Heron Lake | 45 | Easy | Low neck of land from a shallow bay on the north shore |
| 2 | Little Heron Lake → Split Rock Lake | 160 | Moderate | A couple of short climbs; good tread |
| 3 | Split Rock Lake → Cutfoot Lake ("the Cutfoot carry") | 290 | Difficult | Longest portage on the route; steep climb, then a muddy descent |
| 4 | Cutfoot Lake → Gull Lake | 88 | Moderate | Steady grade |
| 5 | Gull Lake → Heron Lake | 130 | Moderate | Long gentle climb, short steep pitch near Heron Lake |

The longest portage on the Heron Chain Loop is the Cutfoot carry, Split Rock Lake → Cutfoot Lake, at 290 rods. It's rated difficult because it is both steep and muddy.

## Campsites

All camping is at designated campsites with a fire grate and latrine, first come, first served.

- **Heron Lake** has 3 designated campsites, useful if you arrive on a late tow.
- **Little Heron Lake** has 1 designated campsite.
- **Split Rock Lake** has 3 designated campsites. The one near the split boulder is the most scenic.
- **Cutfoot Lake** has 2 designated campsites on the north shore, both on rock shelves with great sunset views.
- **Gull Lake** has 4 designated campsites, including two on islands.

**Food storage on this route:** Under the Wilderness Office's food storage rule, bear-resistant containers are required on Heron Lake, Gull Lake and Cutfoot Lake. A food hang is not enough on those three lakes. Elsewhere in the area you may hang food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk instead, but since this loop camps on two of the three required lakes and passes through the third, plan to carry containers for the whole trip.

You may stay at most 3 nights at one campsite. Quiet hours are 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

## Hazards

- **The Cutfoot carry.** Steep, muddy, and long. Wet roots and mud are the main injury risk. Wear boots with good tread, keep loads balanced, and don't rush the descent.
- **Wind on Gull Lake.** Gull Lake's open middle stretch can build real waves in the afternoon. Cross early, stay together, and wait on shore if it's too rough.
- **Remote start and finish.** Because Heron Lake has no road, you depend on the tow boat to get out. Keep track of the day and be at the landing for your pickup.
- **Bears.** The container requirement on Heron, Gull and Cutfoot lakes exists because of bear activity. Cook and store food away from your tent.
- **No cell service.** Bring a satellite messenger so you can call for help or reach us about a pickup change.

## Packing note

Bring bear-resistant containers: they're required on Heron, Gull and Cutfoot lakes, and we rent bear-resistant canisters at the store. Plan enough canisters to hold all your food and scented items for the whole trip. Pack into Portage Pro 60 or Portage Pro 80 packs, and choose the lightest boat you can: a Northline 17 Kevlar (44 lb) makes the Cutfoot carry much more pleasant than the Northline 17 Aluminum (72 lb). Good boots, a water filter, a StormCell 2 stove, and a satellite messenger round out the list. For rental and tow-boat rates, see the current rate sheet.
