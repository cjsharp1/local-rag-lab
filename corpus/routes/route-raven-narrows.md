# Raven Narrows Big Water

*A Paddle Section Supply route guide for the Kettle Basin Canoe Area*

## Overview

Most Kettle Basin routes are about portages. This one is about open water. The Raven Narrows Big Water route takes you from Raven Bay through the Raven Narrows onto Raven Lake, the largest stretch of open water on any of our routes, then over a single portage to Teal Lake. Long horizons, big skies, sandy beaches, and only two portages make it a favorite with sea kayakers and strong canoe teams alike. The price of all that space is wind: on this route, the weather sets your schedule.

- **Length:** 3 days / 2 nights
- **Distance:** 22 miles
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Start and end:** the Raven Bay entry point landing
- **Portages:** 2
- **Longest portage:** Raven Lake → Teal Lake, 70 rods
- **Boats:** kayak-friendly; canoes welcome
- **Main hazard:** wind on big open water

## Getting there

You can reach the Raven Bay landing two ways.

- **Tow boat.** Our tow boat carries you and your boats from the Paddle Section Supply dock on Ashby Lake to the Raven Bay landing. Tow boats leave our dock at 7 a.m., 10 a.m. and 1 p.m. Take the 7 a.m. boat if you can: mornings are usually calmer, and you'll want to be through the narrows before the afternoon wind builds. Stove fuel rides sealed in the tow boat's fuel locker. Reserve at least 48 hours ahead and set your pickup time when you book. For tow-boat costs, see the current rate sheet.
- **Drive.** Raven Bay can also be reached by a rough road. It's slow going, with rocks and washouts; a high-clearance vehicle is strongly recommended, and we don't advise it after heavy rain. Pick up your gear at our store first.

**Permit:** During the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), your overnight permit must be for the **Raven Bay entry point**, and you must enter there on the date printed on it. We issue permits at the counter, and you can pick yours up up to 24 hours before your entry date.

## Day by day

### Day 1: Raven Bay through the narrows to Raven Lake (about 7 miles)

Raven Bay is fairly sheltered, so it's a good place to settle into your boat and check your loads. Paddle north up the bay toward the Raven Narrows, where the shoreline pinches in and the whole of Raven Lake's water funnels through a rocky gap. In any wind the narrows kick up steep, confused waves and a surprising current, so the route uses portage 1, the Narrows bypass, an easy, short trail on the east side of the gap. Even on calm days we recommend using it; conditions can change between the time you see the narrows and the time you're in them.

At the north end of the bypass you'll be on Raven Lake. Follow the east shore to the campsites on the sandy points. Camp here for night one.

### Day 2: Across Raven Lake to Teal Lake (about 6 miles)

Get up early. Raven Lake's main crossing is a long open-water stretch, and the morning is usually the calmest time to make it. If the lake is calm, cross directly to the northwest shore; if there's any chop, follow the shoreline around instead, even though it adds distance. Never start a crossing you're not sure you can finish.

On the northwest shore, portage 2 leaves from a small sand beach and climbs over a low ridge to Teal Lake. It's moderate, with one steady climb; kayak paddlers will want to carry their boats in two trips. Teal Lake is small and sheltered, a welcome change after the big water. Camp on Teal Lake for night two.

### Day 3: Teal Lake back to Raven Bay (about 9 miles)

Carry back over portage 2 to Raven Lake. Instead of retracing yesterday's crossing, follow the sheltered north and east shores of Raven Lake back toward the narrows. This takes a little longer but keeps you near shore for the whole day. Cross the Narrows bypass again and paddle down Raven Bay to the landing. If you came by tow boat, be at the landing for your scheduled pickup. Rental gear is due back at our store by 6 p.m. on your last day.

## Portages

Portage lengths are given in rods: 1 mile = 320 rods; 1 rod = 16.5 ft.

| # | From → To | Length (rods) | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Raven Bay → Raven Lake (Narrows bypass) | 25 | Easy | East side of the narrows; use it in any wind; crossed again on day 3 |
| 2 | Raven Lake → Teal Lake | 70 | Moderate | Longest portage on the route; one steady climb; crossed again on day 3 |

The longest portage on Raven Narrows Big Water is Raven Lake → Teal Lake, at 70 rods. The route has only these two portages; you simply cross each of them in both directions.

## Campsites

All camping is at designated campsites with a fire grate and latrine, first come, first served.

- **Raven Bay** has 2 designated campsites, handy if you arrive late or get windbound on the way out.
- **Raven Lake** has 6 designated campsites, most on sandy points and beaches along the east and north shores. They're exposed, which keeps the bugs down but makes tents vulnerable in a storm. Stake out every guy line.
- **Teal Lake** has 2 designated campsites in the shelter of the pines.

Groups are limited to 9 people and 4 watercraft, and that includes kayaks. You may stay at most 3 nights at one campsite. Quiet hours are 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

## Hazards

- **Wind.** This is the hazard on this route. Raven Lake's long open stretches let waves build quickly, and an afternoon wind can make the lake unsafe for loaded canoes in less than an hour. Travel early, stay near shore, and plan a spare half day in case you're windbound. If the wind is strong when you're due to launch, our staff may delay your start.
- **The Raven Narrows.** Wind and current combine in the gap to make steep, unpredictable waves. Use the bypass.
- **Open crossings.** Make crossings only in calm conditions, stay together, and keep every PFD zipped. Cold water turns a capsize far from shore into an emergency.
- **Lightning.** On big water you're the tallest thing around. Get off the lake at the first sign of thunder.
- **No cell service.** Bring a satellite messenger; it's the only way to reach help or to let us know about a weather delay.
- **Food storage.** Hang food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk, or use a bear-resistant container. On the sandy points, trees for a good hang are scarce, so many paddlers bring a canister.

## Packing note

This route suits kayaks. The Driftline 16 (54 lb) single and Driftline 21 (78 lb) tandem both have rudders that help hold a line in a crosswind, and with only two portages their weight is manageable. Pack in dry bags sized for kayak hatches rather than big portage packs. Canoe paddlers should consider the Northline 17 Kevlar (44 lb) or, for three people, the Wayfarer 18.5 (52 lb), and should keep their loads low and centered. Everyone should bring a spare paddle, a satellite messenger, a bear-resistant canister, a wind shell, and an extra day of food in case you're windbound.
