# Stillwater Traverse

*A Paddle Section Supply route guide for the Kettle Basin Canoe Area*

## Overview

The Stillwater Traverse is our longest route and, for many paddlers, the trip of a lifetime in the Kettle Basin. It crosses the area from the Stillwater Lake put-in to Kettle Lake, one way, through nine portages and a chain of small, little-visited lakes. You'll walk the Bog Walk, camp on lakes that may see only a few groups a week, and finish on the broad water of Kettle Lake. It's long, it's tiring, and it's worth every step.

- **Length:** 6 days / 5 nights
- **Distance:** 44 miles
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Start:** the Stillwater Lake put-in
- **End:** the Kettle Lake landing
- **Portages:** 9
- **Longest portage:** Sorrel Lake → Mossback Lake ("the Bog Walk"), 330 rods

## Getting there

This is a one-way trip. Our van takes you, your boat and your gear from our store in Ashby Landing to the Stillwater Lake put-in, and picks you up at Kettle Lake at the end of day six. The van leaves our dock at 8 a.m. and at noon; take the 8 a.m. van so you have time to reach Lantern Lake on day one. Reserve the shuttle at least 48 hours ahead and set your Kettle Lake pickup time when you book. For shuttle costs, see the current rate sheet.

**Permit:** During the permit quota season (May 1 – September 30), your overnight permit must be for the **Stillwater Lake entry point**, and you must enter there on the date printed on it. Even though you finish at Kettle Lake, the permit is for where you go in, not where you come out. The Stillwater Lake entry point has the smallest daily overnight quota of any entry point we serve, so reserve your permit as early as possible. We issue permits at the counter, and you can pick yours up up to 24 hours before entry.

## Day by day

### Day 1: Stillwater Lake to Lantern Lake (about 8 miles)

Stillwater Lake lives up to its name on most mornings. Paddle its length to the northeast corner, where portage 1, an easy carry, leads to Thimble Pond, a tiny round pond ringed with sedge. Across Thimble Pond, portage 2 is a moderate trail with a couple of rocky climbs over to Lantern Lake. Lantern Lake is long and clear, with campsites on the south shore. Camp here for night one.

### Day 2: Lantern Lake to Sorrel Lake (about 7 miles)

From the east end of Lantern Lake, portage 3 is the first difficult carry of the trip: a steep, rocky climb and a long descent to Crowfoot Lake. Take it slowly and double-portage. Crowfoot Lake is a short paddle, and at its far end portage 4 is an easy, nearly flat stroll to Sorrel Lake. Camp on Sorrel Lake for night two, and rest up: tomorrow is the Bog Walk.

### Day 3: The Bog Walk to Mossback Lake (about 8 miles)

Portage 5, the Bog Walk, is the longest and most famous carry on the route. It crosses a wide spruce-and-tamarack bog on a mix of firm ground, old boardwalk and plain mud. Expect to sink to your ankles in places, and in a wet year deeper. Keep the canoe on your shoulders on the soft sections, test your footing, and stay on the marked line; the bog is easy to wander off. Most groups need much of the morning to finish. Mossback Lake, at the far end, is a quiet, mossy-shored lake with a good campsite on a granite point. Camp here for night three.

### Day 4: Mossback Lake to Quillwort Lake (about 7 miles)

A shorter day to recover. Paddle to the north end of Mossback Lake, where portage 6 climbs moderately over a wooded ridge to Quillwort Lake. Quillwort Lake is shallow and clear, with a sandy bottom along its east shore. Camp here for night four.

### Day 5: Quillwort Lake to Slate Pond (about 8 miles)

From Quillwort Lake, portage 7 is a long, moderate trail through mixed forest to Ember Lake, named for its red-tinted cliffs at sunset. Cross Ember Lake to portage 8, the shortest carry of the trip, an easy hop to Slate Pond. Camp on Slate Pond for night five.

### Day 6: Slate Pond to Kettle Lake (about 6 miles)

The last portage is a hard one. Portage 9 is rated difficult, with a steep climb out of Slate Pond and a long, rocky descent to Kettle Lake. Once you're on Kettle Lake, paddle to the Kettle Lake landing to meet our van at your scheduled time. Kettle Lake is the only lake in the area where motors are allowed (up to 25 hp), so watch for motorboats and keep to the shoreline. Rental gear is due back at our store by 6 p.m. on your last day.

## Campsites

All camping is at designated campsites with a fire grate and latrine, first come, first served.

- **Stillwater Lake:** 2 designated campsites.
- **Lantern Lake:** 3 designated campsites on the south shore.
- **Crowfoot Lake:** 1 designated campsite.
- **Sorrel Lake:** 2 designated campsites.
- **Mossback Lake:** 2 designated campsites, including the granite point site.
- **Quillwort Lake:** 2 designated campsites.
- **Ember Lake:** 1 designated campsite.
- **Slate Pond:** 1 designated campsite.

Because several lakes on this route have only one or two sites, have a backup plan for each night. If Slate Pond's single site is taken, Ember Lake's is the nearest fallback. Groups are limited to 9 people and 4 watercraft. Quiet hours are 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

## Hazards

- **The Bog Walk.** Deep mud, slick boardwalk and an easy-to-lose trail. Never leave anyone behind on the bog, and don't try it late in the day.
- **Cumulative fatigue.** Nine portages over six days add up. Three of them, including the Bog Walk, are rated difficult. Eat well, drink plenty of water, and don't push into the evening.
- **Remoteness.** The middle of this route is the most remote country we send people into. There's no cell service anywhere along it. Carry a satellite messenger and a first-aid kit, and know how to use both.
- **Motorboats on Kettle Lake.** After days of quiet, the boat traffic on Kettle Lake can be a surprise. Stay near shore and be visible.
- **Food storage.** Hang food 12 ft up and 6 ft out from the trunk, or use a bear-resistant container.

## Packing note

Weight is everything on this trip. Choose the Northline 17 Kevlar (44 lb), or the Kestrel 15 (32 lb) if you're traveling solo, and leave the Northline 17 Aluminum at home. Pack into two Portage Pro 80 packs per canoe, bring a food barrel or bear-resistant canister sized for six days, and choose boots you don't mind ruining in the bog. Add a satellite messenger, a water filter, a StormCell 2 stove with enough fuel for the whole trip, and a spare paddle. No cans or glass bottles.
